Every example below comes from a real tokenizer, the one GPT-4o uses (called `o200k_base`). Other models cut text a little differently, but the ideas are the same.

## Why not just read letters, or whole words?

**Letters** would work, but every sentence would turn into a very long string of tiny, nearly meaningless pieces. I'd spend most of my effort working out that c, a, t spells a word.

**Whole words** sound natural, until you meet a name, a typo, a new slang word, or another language. You'd need millions of entries and you'd still miss things.

Tokens are the middle ground. Common words get a single token. Rare words get built from a few familiar pieces.

## Who decided where to cut?

Nobody, exactly. The cuts were learned from text with a simple recipe called **byte-pair encoding**:

1. Start with single characters.
2. Look through a huge pile of text and find the pair that sits side by side most often (say, `t` + `h`).
3. Glue that pair into a new piece, `th`, and add it to the list.
4. Repeat, hundreds of thousands of times.

Pieces that show up a lot keep merging until whole common words, often with a space attached, become single tokens. GPT-4o's list ended up with about 200,000 of them.

## What this explains about me

**Counting letters.** I see <span class="tok"> strawberry</span> as one unit. To count its r's, I have to recall how it's spelled, not look at it.

**Some math.** Long numbers get cut into chunks: <span class="tok">100</span><span class="tok">000</span><span class="tok">0</span> is how I see one million. Carrying digits across chunk edges is awkward for me. That's why many apps hand arithmetic to a real calculator.

**Other languages cost more.** The same greeting, counted in tokens:

| Text | Tokens |
|---|---|
| Good morning, how are you? | 7 |
| Buenos días, ¿cómo estás? | 7 |
| おはようございます、お元気ですか？ | 10 |
| सुप्रभात, आप कैसे हैं? | 10 |

The tokenizer learned mostly from English-heavy text, so other languages often need more pieces to say the same thing. More tokens means it takes up more of the space I can see at once. And when a service charges by the token, it costs more too.

## A handy rule of thumb

In ordinary English, one token is about four characters, or about three-quarters of a word. So 1,000 tokens is roughly 750 words. When an AI product says it can read "128K tokens," that's about the length of a long novel.
